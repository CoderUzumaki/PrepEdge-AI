## A. Recruiter screening call — ~30 seconds

> Hi [Recruiter], I'm Abhinav Mishra — a final-year Computer Science student at NIT
> Raipur, graduating in 2027. Right now I'm interning as a backend engineer at NPT
> Solution, building FastAPI services for a multi-tenant healthcare SaaS. Outside of

> around 30 GitHub stars and a small contributor community. I'm looking for a
> New-Grad SDE role that begins with a six-month internship and converts into a
> full-time offer — and I'd love to understand what that path looks like at

**Why this works:** Front-loads school + graduation year (recruiter's first filter),
lands one crisp signal of technical depth (FastAPI multi-tenant SaaS), one signal of
initiative (PrepEdge AI), and closes with the exact ask so the recruiter knows how to
help you.

a single box.

**Why this works:** Opens with a *thesis* ("shipping things that outlive me") that
gives the bar-raiser a hook to hang your STAR stories on. Every subsequent
question — *tell me about a time you took ownership / disagreed / delivered under
pressure* — now maps back to your stated thesis. Uses the intro as strategic framing,
not just context.

> Hi, I'm Abhinav Mishra — final-year CS at NIT Raipur, graduating in 2027, and
> currently interning as a backend engineer at NPT Solution. Beyond the code itself,
> three things describe how I like to work.
>

> the accountable person when it breaks. Second, I try to communicate clearly, which

> committee. And third, I'm allergic to shipping without measuring — I'd rather
> ship less and know the outcome than ship more and hope.
>

**Why this works:** HR / cultural rounds are looking for values expressed in concrete
behaviors. This intro converts three abstract values (ownership, communication,
measurement) into three specific résumé anchors — no hand-waving, no adjectives. It
also gracefully reframes the Documentation Executive role (which could look
non-technical) as evidence of a communication value.

## H. LinkedIn / cold outreach — written, 3 sentences

> Hi [Name], I'm Abhinav — a final-year CS student at NIT Raipur, currently interning
> on the FastAPI backend team at NPT Solution's PawsPal Connect. I've been following
> [Company]'s work on [specific product / team], and I wanted to reach out because
> I'm exploring New-Grad SDE roles for a mid-2026 internship converting to a 2027
> full-time start — is there an appropriate contact or process you'd point me
> toward? For context, my open-source project PrepEdge AI (~34 stars) and my LeetCode
> Knight profile are linked on my page, and I'd be grateful for any pointer, even
> if it's just the name of the right person to reach out to next.

**Why this works:** Follows the *warm-cold outreach* pattern: (1) who you are in one
line, (2) evidence you actually know their work, (3) the ask, (4) proof-of-work links
they can verify without you begging for a call. Never asks for a job directly — asks
for a pointer, which has a far higher reply rate. Keep total length under 90 words.

## Delivery notes (read before your first mock)

1. **Never memorize word-for-word.** Memorize the *shape* — the beats, the order, the
   anchor facts. Interviewers can tell when you're reciting.
2. **Cut fillers.** *"So, basically, I'm currently..."* → *"I'm currently..."*.
3. **End with silence, not a trail.** After your intro, stop. Let the interviewer
   drive the next turn. Trailing off with *"...yeah, that's pretty much me"*
   signals lack of confidence.
4. **Update your one number.** Every intro that cites the star count or LeetCode
   rating should be refreshed the day before an interview. A stale metric is worse
   than no metric.
5. **Match energy to the room.** Intro `F` (founder) should feel warmer and more
   conversational than `D` (system design), even if the words look similar on paper.
6. **Practice against a timer.** Record yourself. If `A` runs over 45 seconds or `I`
   runs over 20, cut — don't slow down.
